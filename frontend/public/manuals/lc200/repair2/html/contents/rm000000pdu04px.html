Advanced cam timing:
With a warm engine and an engine speed of between 500 rpm and 4,000 rpm, conditions (a), (b) and (c) are met (1 trip detection logic):
  1. (a) The difference between the target and actual intake valve timings is more than 5°CA (crankshaft angle) for 4.5 seconds.
  2. (b) The current intake valve timing is fixed (timing changes less than 5°CA in 5 seconds).
  3. (c) Variations in the VVT controller timing are more than 19°CA of the maximum delayed timing (malfunction in advance timing).
  1. Valve timing
  2. Camshaft Oil Control Valve (OCV)
  3. OCV filter
  4. Camshaft timing gear assembly
  5. ECM

Retarded cam timing:
With a warm engine and an engine speed of between 500 rpm and 4000 rpm, conditions (a), (b) and (c) are met (2 trip detection logic):
  1. (a) The difference between the target and actual intake valve timings is more than 5°CA (crankshaft angle) for 4.5 seconds.
  2. (b) The current intake valve timing is fixed (timing changes less than 5°CA in 5 seconds).
  3. (c) Variations in the VVT controller timing are 19°CA or less of the maximum delayed timing (malfunction in retarded timing).

MONITOR DESCRIPTION

  1. The ECM optimizes the intake valve timing using the VVT (Variable Valve Timing) system to control the intake camshaft. The VVT system includes the ECM, the camshaft Oil Control Valve (OCV) and the VVT controller. The ECM sends a target duty-cycle control signal to the OCV. This control signal regulates the oil pressure supplied to the VVT controller. The VVT controller can advance or retard the intake camshaft.
  2. If the difference between the target and actual intake valve timings is large, and changes in the actual intake valve timing are small, the ECM interprets this as the VVT controller stuck malfunction and stores a DTC.
  1. Example:
  2. A DTC is stored when conditions 1, 2 and 3 are met:
    1. 1. The difference between the target and actual intake valve timing is more than 5°CA (Crankshaft Angle) and the condition continues for more than 4.5 seconds.
    2. 2. It takes 5 seconds or more to change the valve timing by 5°CA.
    3. 3. After conditions 1 and 2 are met, the OCV is forcibly activated 63 times or more.
  3. DTC P0011 and P0021 (Advanced Cam Timing) are subject to 1 trip detection logic.
  4. DTC P0012 and P0022 (Retarded Cam Timing) are subject to 2 trip detection logic.
  5. These DTCs indicate that the VVT controller cannot operate properly due to OCV malfunctions or the presence of foreign objects in the OCV.
  6. The monitor will run if all of the following conditions are met:
    1. The engine is warm (the engine coolant temperature is 75°C [167°F] or more).
    2. The vehicle has been driven at more than 64 km/h (40 mph) for 3 minutes.
    3. The engine has idled for 3 minutes.

  1. DTC P0011, P0012, P0021 or P0022 may be stored when foreign objects in the engine oil are caught in some parts of the system. The DTC will remain stored even if the system returns to normal after a short time. Foreign objects are filtered out by the oil filter.
  2. If DTC P0011 or P0012 is output, check the right bank VVT system for the intake camshaft circuit (bank 1).
  3. Bank 1 refers to the bank that includes the No. 1 cylinder.
  4. If DTC P0021 or P0022 is output, check the left bank VVT system for the intake camshaft circuit (bank 2).
  5. Bank 2 refers to the bank that does not include the No. 1 cylinder.
  6. Read freeze frame data using the intelligent tester. Freeze frame data records the engine condition when malfunctions are detected. When troubleshooting, freeze frame data can help determine if the vehicle was moving or stationary, if the engine was warmed up or not, if the air-fuel ratio was lean or rich, and other data from the time the malfunction occurred.
